Shipping AI skill assessment into a live product

Problem
Completion tracked whether students had moved through content, not whether they could actually do the work. For healthcare certification programs, that gap matters — a credential has to mean something to an employer.
What I did
Designed an AI-powered skill assessment and the milestone-unlock workflow behind it, so progression depends on demonstrated competency rather than time spent. Took it from concept to a live, student-facing feature.
Tradeoff
Gating progression risks friction and drop-off. I prioritised getting the assessment criteria right with coach input over shipping across every program at once, and accepted a slower rollout to protect the student experience.
Outcome
The feature met the outcomes we set for it and is now being rolled out across additional bootcamps.

Turning coaching sessions into an AI agent

Problem
Live coaching is the most expensive part of delivering a bootcamp, and it doesn't scale. The same questions get answered by a paid expert over and over, cohort after cohort.
What I did
Built an AI agent on Tavus using recorded coaching sessions as source material — structuring them into a knowledge base, then generating a conversational agent students can question directly, so expert time goes to what genuinely needs a human.
Tradeoff
An agent that answers confidently but wrongly is worse than no agent at all in a healthcare program. I scoped it tightly to material the coaches had actually covered rather than letting it range across the whole subject.
Outcome
A working agent aimed squarely at the largest variable cost in the portfolio.

Launching two bootcamps end to end

Problem
Two new programs had to go from an idea to something students could enrol in — no content, no curriculum, nobody hired to teach them.
What I did
Owned both launches end to end: scoped the programs, built the content, and sourced, evaluated and hired the subject-matter experts who deliver them.
Tradeoff
Hiring the right expert is slow, and the cheapest candidate is rarely the right one. In a healthcare program the instructor's clinical credibility is the product, so I optimised for that over speed to launch.
Outcome
Both programs launched and now sit inside the portfolio I run, taught by the experts I brought in.
