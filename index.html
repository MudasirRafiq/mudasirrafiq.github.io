Shipping AI skill assessment into a live product

Problem
Completion tracked whether students had moved through content, not whether they could actually do the work. For healthcare certification programs, that gap matters — a credential has to mean something to an employer.
What I did
Designed an AI-powered skill assessment and the milestone-unlock workflow behind it, so progression depends on demonstrated competency rather than time spent. Took it from concept to a live, student-facing feature.
Tradeoff
Gating progression risks friction and drop-off. I prioritised getting the assessment criteria right with coach input over shipping across every program at once, and accepted a slower rollout to protect the student experience.
Outcome
The feature met the outcomes we set for it and is now being rolled out across additional bootcamps.

The AI SME — an expert built from 80 coaching sessions

Problem
Live coaching is the most expensive part of delivering a bootcamp, and it doesn't scale. The same questions get answered by a paid subject-matter expert over and over, cohort after cohort.
What I did
Mined roughly 80 recorded coaching sessions from the Surgical Technologist program for the questions students actually ask, expanded them into a twelve-document knowledge base, and stood up a conversational AI SME on Tavus that answers them directly — so expert time goes to what genuinely needs a human.
Tradeoff
An agent that answers confidently but wrongly is worse than no agent at all in a healthcare program. I scoped it tightly to material the coaches had actually covered rather than letting it range across the whole subject, and designed the flow so the student opens the conversation rather than the agent — which also keeps usage costs down.
Outcome
Through QA and demoed internally, with a modelled cut in coaching cost per student substantial enough to change the economics of the program.

Three AI voice agents, including billing

Problem
Two different phone problems, both eating staff hours: inbound leads going cold because nobody called back fast enough, and payment follow-up that someone had to work through by hand.
What I did
Built three voice agents on ElevenLabs in parallel — a B2C lead-response agent for inbound and outbound calls, a workforce-development variant scoped to exclude pricing, and a billing agent for payment follow-up, specified down to call transcripts for nine distinct outcome codes plus a student FAQ script.
Tradeoff
A billing call is the one conversation where getting it wrong costs trust, not just time. Rather than letting the agent improvise, I wrote the script for every outcome it could land on, which meant a slower build than a general-purpose agent.
Outcome
The billing agent is built and in testing. I now own the company's full AI agent stack — the Tavus personas, prompts and knowledge bases, and the ElevenLabs agents with their telephony setup.
